🔍 A "Burnt Siu Mai" by the Window? It Was a Highly Aggressive Hornet Nest

Why does the nest look like a siu mai? Hornets chew wood fibres mixed with saliva into a papery material, building the shell layer by layer — the result is a grey-brown, paper-like surface covered in wrinkled swirls that genuinely resembles a burnt dumpling from afar. Amusing as it looked, our team's professional assessment confirmed it was a highly aggressive hornet nest. Hornets (such as the black-tailed hornet) carry a potent sting that injects far more venom than a common wasp, and this nest sat extremely close to a window the residents open and close daily — one accidental disturbance could provoke a mass attack. The risk was absolutely not to be taken lightly.

Many people's first instinct is to "spray it with insecticide" or "knock it down with a coat hanger" — but these are extremely dangerous moves. A startled colony swarms out at once: at best residents get stung, at worst the whole swarm pours indoors. The risk multiplies when children, elderly family members or anyone allergic to wasp venom is at home. Here is how a professional nest-removal team handled this Mei Shing Court case safely and thoroughly.

🌅 Morning Nest Removal: Full Protection, Decisive Action

To lift the burden off the resident as quickly as possible, our technical supervisor moved swiftly and launched the operation that very morning. Wasps are more active in daytime, but with the master's extensive field experience, the right gear and the right insecticides, the operation remained safe and efficient. The removal followed four steps:

1. Full protective gear: The master put on the highest-grade bee-keeping suit — hood, gloves and overshoes all sealed — leaving the colony no gap to exploit from head to toe.

2. Fast-acting spray blockade: A professional wasp spray with rapid knockdown agents precisely sealed the nest's entrance and exits, paralyzing the hornets inside within moments and greatly reducing the chance of a startled swarm rushing out.

3. Whole-nest extraction + sealed packing: Once the colony lost its ability to attack, the master skillfully removed the entire "siu mai" nest roots and all, packing it straight into a dedicated sealed bag to stop any remaining wasps from escaping and stinging again.

4. Residual insecticide against rebuilding: A long-lasting repellent residual insecticide was applied around the original nest site on the balcony, destroying the pheromone scent marks the colony had left behind and reducing the chance of wasps returning to rebuild on the same spot.

🐝 A Light Moment: The Lone Hornet Circled — Then Flew Away

Just a few minutes after the nest was removed, a lone hornet — returning from a foraging trip outside — flew back. It circled the balcony spot for a while, as if searching for a home that was no longer there, then simply flew away.

"No need to worry — this actually proves our residual insecticide is working!" the master explained on the spot. "The nest itself has been completely removed, and the protective agent has covered up the original pheromone scent marks. This homeless hornet realises its 'home' is gone and it can't rebuild here, so it has no choice but to fly off."

This little episode is exactly why step 4 matters: removing the nest is only half the job. Only by wiping out the scent marks as well can we make sure lone returning wasps won't linger at the spot — or start a brand-new nest.

⚠️ Wasp or Hornet — and Why You Should Never Handle It Yourself?

Many people struggle to tell wasps and hornets apart. Put simply, hornets are usually larger than common wasps, with double the aggression and toxicity — you can start from three signs:

  • Size: hornets are a whole size larger than common wasps — one look tells you which is which;
  • Aggression: hornets are fiercer with a wider alert zone; disturb the nest and the colony may chase as a group;
  • Toxicity: a single hornet sting injects far more venom than a common wasp, and multiple stings can trigger severe allergic reactions.

Whether it's a wasp nest or a hornet nest, we strongly warn against watching an online tutorial and boldly tackling it yourself. Careless use of insecticide spray, fire, or poking the nest with a coat hanger easily enrages the colony — scattering it and driving the wasps straight indoors, with irreversible consequences for personal safety. A professional nest-removal team not only has the protective equipment, but also knows how to choose the right insecticide and technique for the species and the site — that is the safest way.
