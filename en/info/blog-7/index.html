Spotting cockroaches, rats or bed bugs at home is a real headache! You want a professional pest control company to come and sort it out, but you worry about being ripped off? Today we break down how pest control services across Hong Kong structure their fees and what the latest market budget looks like — so you know exactly what to expect!

2026 Hong Kong Pest Control Market: Pricing Overview

According to combined statistics from major local service-matching platforms (data reference: PRO360), a single pest control service visit in Hong Kong currently costs roughly HK$750 to HK$8,000.

Actual quotes are adjusted according to the following four factors:

  • Severity of the infestation (a few stray pests or an established nest)
  • Usable floor area of the flat (pricing tiers are usually set by square footage)
  • Number of treatments and chemicals used (one-off eradication or a regular maintenance plan)
  • Type of pest (different pests require completely different techniques and chemicals)

Common Pest Treatments: Market Reference Price List

Most pest control companies charge based on the usable floor area (square footage) of the flat. Below is a summary of the typical price ranges for the main pests on the market today, to help you budget:

1. Cockroach / Ant Treatment Prices

  • Small flats under 300 sq ft: about HK$900 - HK$1,000 / visit
  • Mid-sized flats of 300-700 sq ft: about HK$1,000 - HK$1,800 / visit
  • Large flats over 700 sq ft: from HK$1,800 / visit

Note: quotes may be adjusted slightly if you live near hillsides or have a lot of clutter.

2. Termite Treatment Prices

Termites are extremely destructive to wooden furniture and structures, and the treatment process is complex, so prices are relatively higher:

  • Flats under 300 sq ft: about HK$2,800 / visit
  • Flats of 300-700 sq ft: about HK$2,800 - HK$3,600 / visit
  • Flats over 700 sq ft: about HK$3,600 - HK$8,000 / visit

3. Bed Bug / Book Lice Treatment Prices

Bed bugs are extremely stubborn and often require more precise application techniques:

  • Flats under 300 sq ft: about HK$3,000 - HK$3,700 / visit
  • Flats of 300-700 sq ft: about HK$3,700 - HK$5,800 / visit
  • Flats over 700 sq ft: from HK$5,800 / visit

4. Rat (Rodent) Control Prices

  • Flats under 300 sq ft: about HK$1,900 - HK$2,500 / visit
  • Flats of 300-700 sq ft: about HK$2,500 - HK$4,000 / visit
  • Flats over 700 sq ft: from HK$4,000 / visit

Important tip: if the rodent problem is severe, several extra visits or a long-term follow-up maintenance plan are usually needed, costing around HK$2,000 - HK$15,000.

How to Get the Most Accurate Quote from a Pest Control Expert?

Want a precise quote with no surprise mark-ups when the technician arrives? Provide the following information when you enquire:

  • Take photos or videos: capture exactly where the pests appear, how many there are, and any droppings they leave behind.
  • Describe your home: tell the technician the approximate square footage, the layout, and whether you have a lot of built-in furniture.
  • Mention any DIY treatments: if you have previously used insecticide sprays bought from chain stores, be sure to tell the technician — certain chemicals can make pests develop pesticide resistance or scatter, affecting professional treatment.

3 Essential Safety and Anti-Scam Tips for Hiring a Pest Control Company

Finally, remember: besides price, the most important things to check when hiring a pest control service are:

  • Check the licence: under Hong Kong law (the Pest Control Products Ordinance), a qualified pest control company must hold a pesticide licence issued by the Agriculture, Fisheries and Conservation Department (AFCD) — your assurance that chemicals are used safely.
  • Human and pet safety: before treatment, ask about the chemical ingredients (for example, pyrethrin, which is friendly to children and pets) and follow the technician's instructions strictly. After treatment the whole flat usually needs 4 to 6 hours of ventilation before re-entry.
  • Warranty period: social pests like termites and bed bugs are hard to eliminate 100% in one go. Before signing, ask clearly whether the technician provides regular follow-up visits or after-service maintenance.

Frequently Asked Questions about Pest Control Companies (Q&A)

Below are the questions consumers ask most often when hiring a pest control company, answered with professional information:

How do I choose a professional pest control company?

When choosing a pest control company, remember to consider these six key factors:

  • Rich experience: an experienced company understands all kinds of pests in depth and can devise the most effective treatment plan for different situations.
  • Valid licence: pest control requires specialised chemicals. Under the Pest Control Products Ordinance issued by the AFCD, a pest control company must hold a pesticide licence before it can sell such chemicals and carry out treatment work.
  • Reviews and reputation: check online reviews, read up on the company, or ask friends and family. A well-reviewed company gives you better assurance of results.
  • Safe methods and chemicals: make sure the company's methods and chemicals comply with the relevant safety regulations.
  • Service coverage and pricing: confirm the company serves your area and that its charges are reasonable.
  • After-sales service: a company that offers after-sales service ensures any follow-up issues during or after treatment are properly handled.
Do pest control companies provide regular follow-up services?

Yes. Pests — especially social ones such as ants and termites — usually need a period of follow-up before they can be brought under control, so pest control companies provide regular follow-up services to resolve the problem completely for customers.

Can I arrange regular preventive treatments with a pest control company?

Yes. As the saying goes, prevention is better than cure — keeping pests out of your home in the first place is the most fundamental approach. You can engage a pest control company regularly for preventive treatments to stop pests breeding in large numbers indoors.

Are pest control services safe for pets and children?

Yes. The specialised chemicals pest control companies use mainly target pests. Pyrethrin, for example, has minimal effect on humans and pets, but it is still toxic, so during treatment everyone except the technicians — including pets — should leave the premises.

Do I need to move out during the pest control treatment?

No. Although people must leave the premises during treatment, from the start of work until it is safe to go back in usually takes only about 4 to 6 hours, so there is no need to move out. Before treatment, simply cover furniture surfaces with masking film, cling film or newspaper and wait for the technician to arrive.

Do I need a pest control company when moving into a new home?

Yes. Although new buildings look clean and fresh, hidden risks can remain — for example, wood can easily harbour termites or wood borers. Early pest prevention protects both the structure and the renovation of your new home, and ensures a hygienic, comfortable environment when you move in.

After the treatment, when can I move back in?

Generally you can go home right after the treatment. However, if fogging (smoke treatment) was used, wait 4 to 5 hours after spraying before opening the windows to ventilate, and only move in once the chemicals in the air have fully dispersed — this avoids inhaling too much chemical and feeling unwell.

How do I know if my home has bed bugs?

Bed bugs are small, avoid light and love hiding in the tiniest cracks. You can check with these three methods:

  • Check books: look for brown or dark-red spots on the pages.
  • Shine a torch on clutter and cracks: look for bed bug faecal marks or their shed skins.
  • Inspect upholstered furniture closely: these are among the favourite hiding places of bed bugs and similar insects.
Does alcohol kill bed bugs?

Yes. Alcohol at a concentration of 70% or above can kill bed bugs.

Does insecticide spray kill bed bugs?

Common chemical insecticide sprays can kill bed bugs, but bed bugs develop pesticide resistance easily. Never spray indiscriminately — it only strengthens their resistance and makes the problem even harder to solve.

Are cockroaches afraid of camphor oil?

Yes. Experiments confirm that the smell of camphor oil keeps cockroaches away, so it can be used as a repellent. However, if anyone in your household has G6PD deficiency (favism) or thalassaemia, this method must not be used at all. Camphor oil also evaporates easily — spray it once a day to maintain the effect.

What should I do about fire ants at home?

Fire ants at home must be dealt with immediately — they not only damage property but can also bite people. Here are seven ways to respond:

  • Locate the nest: first find where they are nesting. Fire ant mounds usually form small hill-shaped piles of soil, and they sometimes nest under walls or floor slabs.
  • Use ant bait: buy bait made specifically for fire ants and place it near the nest. The bait contains an attractant plus a slow-acting poison; worker ants carry it back into the nest and share it with the colony, wiping out the whole nest.
  • Spray insecticide: for outdoor nests, spray insecticide directly. Choose a product effective against fire ants and follow the instructions for safe use.
  • Seal entry points: check cracks and holes in your home and seal them with caulk to stop fire ants crawling back in, especially window frames and fine wall cracks.
  • Keep the environment clean: clear away food scraps and rubbish promptly (especially in the kitchen) and avoid standing water and damp.
  • Inspect regularly: even after the nest is destroyed, keep checking; treat again at the first sign of a recurrence.
  • Seek professional help: if you cannot manage it yourself or the colony is very large, call in a professional pest control company.
Are rats afraid of mothballs?

Yes. The fumes from mothballs are highly unpleasant to rats and drive them away.

If I catch a rat, should I release it?

Releasing it is not recommended. Release spreads the rodent problem to other places, transmits germs, and the rat will most likely find its way straight back into your home.

Quick ways to dispose of a caught rat include:

  • Rat traps / snap traps: simple and direct — the rat is dealt with instantly.
  • Poison bait: place it where rats are active (but mind the safety of people and pets to avoid accidental ingestion).
  • Glue boards: trap the rat on the board, then dispose of it.
  • Professional rodent control: engage a professional pest control company to make sure the rodent problem is solved completely.
Are termites harmful to humans?

In nature, termites are actually beneficial insects that help break down rotting branches and leaves; they are non-toxic and do not attack people. But because they love eating wood and destroying buildings, they can cause tremendous damage to our lives and property.

Are termites affected by bleach?

Termites do react to bleach, but using bleach to drive them away is absolutely the wrong approach. Its pungent smell only forces termites to move temporarily and build a new nest somewhere even better hidden, which makes treatment far harder. So if you find termites, never spray chemicals around or move the furniture — you will only alert them and send them scattering.

Where do fleas hide?

Fleas love dark, humid environments. Besides on cats and dogs, fleas and their eggs and larvae most often hide in these seven places:

  • In or under carpets
  • Cracks in the floor
  • Wall corners
  • On pets
  • Pet beds/nests and the surrounding floor
  • Indoor corners
  • Places where dust tends to gather
Is termite treatment chemical toxic?

Yes. Termite treatments usually contain boric acid and similar ingredients — handle them carefully to avoid accidental ingestion or poisoning.

How do I make homemade termite bait?

Mix 5 to 10 grams of boric acid with banana (including the peel) blended into a paste, then stir the two together evenly. Bury the mixture in soil or near wood to attract termites to feed — this achieves a termite-killing effect.
