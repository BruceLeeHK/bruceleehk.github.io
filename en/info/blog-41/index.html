🔍 The Scene: a “Kitchen Disaster” in Kowloon Tong's Low-Density Estate Area

Kowloon Tong and the Oxford Road area are low-density residential districts where gardens and yards back onto drains and green belts — exactly the active zone of the sewer rat (Norway rat, Rattus norvegicus): hiding in the underground drains by day, climbing up the pipework at night to forage in the garden, then entering the house through wall-pipe gaps, drain openings and door gaps by the kitchen. In this case the giant sewer rat didn't stop at the garden — it went straight into the kitchen and chewed through the fridge water pipe and the dishwasher water pipe. Rats must gnaw day and night to wear their teeth down, and a plastic water pipe is the perfect “chew toy”; once bitten through, the leak can flood the kitchen or short-circuit the fridge and dishwasher. The risk is real.

Many overseas owners' first thought is “just have the helper buy some rat poison” — but in a home with a dog, that road is a dead end. Here's why.

⚠️ The Biggest Constraint: Rat Control in a Golden Retriever Household

What worried Mrs Hon most was the family's beloved Golden Retriever. With traditional rodenticides (such as highly toxic anticoagulants), if the dog accidentally ate the bait — or caught a poisoned rat wandering around (secondary poisoning) — the consequences could be devastating. So “absolute safety for the dog” was the non-negotiable top principle of this mission, and the market's default “just lay poison” approach was completely off the table.

At the WhatsApp remote assessment stage we told the owner clearly: with pets at home the method must be chosen with care — the chemicals that may be used and where they may be placed are strictly limited, and baiting may simply be unsuitable. We recommended trapping as the main approach, with the helper managing the dog's range (kept in one room when she is home, walked by the helper when she goes out) — so rodent control and pet safety can both be achieved.

🛠️ WhatsApp Remote Diagnosis × the 3-Visit Toxic-Free Physical Trapping Plan

After detailed cross-border WhatsApp discussions on the structure and on-site photos, we quickly tailored a “3-visit toxic-free physical trapping plan” for Mrs Hon in Singapore:

  • Zero-chemical safe setup: no rodenticide at any point — the indoor air and environment stay 100% harmless to the Golden Retriever;
  • Precision physical trapping: against the high vigilance of the sewer rats common in Kowloon Tong's low-density homes, we skipped ordinary cages that fail easily and used high-tack professional glue boards and concealed traps, precisely positioned along the rats' runways;
  • Pet-safety coordination at home: we gave the helper detailed guidance on simple physical separation during the work (area barriers, room management) so the Golden Retriever stays in a safe zone — both goals met at once.

After confirming the Oxford Road address and assessing the two key activity zones — kitchen and garden — the owner placed the order for the 3-visit plan on the spot and shared the key clue: “I think they come up from the drains.” That matched our remote assessment exactly: sewer rats rise through the drainage network, build their runways in the garden first, then enter the kitchen for water and food. Control the runway and you control the rat — so the setup went along drain openings, pipe runs and garden paths.

The sewer rat hiding in this Kowloon Tong home was extremely cunning and alert — it even avoided the ordinary traps sold in shops. But with years of field experience and precise runway analysis, this “wrecker” fell into our professional physical trapping device late at night on Day 2 after work began. Mrs Hon and her helper could finally relax, and the kitchen pipe crisis was fully resolved.

❓ Pet-Household & Overseas-Owner Rat Control FAQ

Can we still use rat poison if we have a Golden Retriever / pets at home?

Not recommended. Highly toxic anticoagulant bait is a double risk for pets: the dog may eat the bait directly, and a poisoned rat wanders around before it dies — if your dog catches that “poisoned rat”, it suffers secondary poisoning. Pet households should switch to toxic-free physical trapping (professional glue boards + concealed traps) with physical separation (area barriers / room management), which is 100% safe for the dog.

I'm overseas (Singapore etc.) — how can I deal with a rat problem in my Hong Kong home?

Use WhatsApp remote diagnosis: send photos of the environment + short videos + a description; the master identifies the species, runways and entry points, tailors a plan and guides your domestic helper, and daily battle reports come back once the work starts. We also offer an Overseas Owner Remote Service — handover and pre-tenancy treatment are covered too.

Rats have chewed through the fridge / dishwasher water pipe — what should I do?

Shut the main water valve and cut power to the affected appliances immediately, take photos for the record, and arrange a plumber to repair the pipes. Most importantly, root-treat the rat infestation at the same time — sewer rats gnaw day and night, so without control the new pipes will be chewed through again, and chewed wiring can cause short circuits or even a fire.

What is a sewer rat? Why do ordinary rat cages from the shops fail?

A “sewer rat” is the Norway rat (brown rat), which rises from the underground drainage network. It is highly alert and neophobic — it avoids newly placed cages. It takes high-tack glue boards + concealed traps, precisely positioned along the runways, plus pre-baiting and position adjustment to catch one — exactly how this Kowloon Tong case was solved on Day 2.

How long did this Kowloon Tong case take? How many visits are typical?

In this case the giant sewer rat was caught late at night on Day 2 after work began. Typically we arrange 1–3 follow-up visits depending on the environment, with a trapping period checked every 2 weeks to 1 month, depending on infestation density and the structure of the site; a reputable company states the follow-up arrangement in the quotation.

With glue boards / traps, how do you make sure the Golden Retriever won't get stuck?

We run a pet-safety assessment before the work: traps go only on runways the dog cannot reach (behind cabinets, at drain openings, along garden fencing); during the trapping period the helper is guided to separate the dog by room / barrier so it stays in a safe zone; and all traps are collected when the follow-up ends — nothing is left behind in the home.
