A row of black specks on top of the kitchen cabinet, debris along the skirting board — everyone's first move is searching "rat droppings chart" or "roach poop ID". Misidentify the culprit and you buy the wrong treatment: rat droppings suggest a structural entry problem; cockroach droppings mean a colony is already breeding nearby. This guide compares the two most common "crime scene traces" across 5 features, then gives you the 3 correct clean-up steps.

🐭 What Do Rat Droppings Look Like?

Rat droppings are spindle- or rice-shaped, 6–12mm long (Norway rats produce larger pellets than house mice). Fresh ones are dark black, moist and shiny; older ones turn grey and matte. The giveaway is distribution: rats travel along wall-side "runways", so droppings pile up in corners, on cabinet tops, inside ceiling voids and storage areas — one rat can leave 50+ pellets a day. A pile means more than one rat. For treatment, go straight to our rat control company hub.

🪳 And Cockroach Droppings?

Cockroach droppings are far smaller: 1–2mm, like black pepper or coarse coffee grounds — short cylindrical specks, dark brown to black, dry-hard and slightly sticky, stuck to cabinet corners, drawer rails, appliance seams and under sinks. German cockroach (the most common home species) frass also forms black "stain clusters" in corners. Expect oothecae (egg cases), moulted skins and a sour smell nearby — if you can smell a "fermented" odour, the colony is close. Treatment options on our cockroach control page (gel-bait colony treatment is the way to end it).

⚠️ Health Risks: Why You Should Never Dry-Sweep

Rat droppings are not just gross: hantavirus can infect via dust raised from dried rodent urine and droppings, alongside salmonella and leptospirosis. Cockroach frass and moults are potent allergens linked to childhood asthma. So the first response to droppings is not a broom — it is a spray bottle: dry-sweeping launches pathogens and allergens straight into the air.

✅ The 3 Correct Clean-up Steps

  • ① Spray-wet and disinfect — mist the droppings and surroundings with 1:49 diluted bleach, leave at least 5 minutes so pathogens deactivate in the moisture;
  • ② Wear gloves and clean — pick up with disposable gloves and paper towel or a one-use scoop; double-bag and seal before disposal; never touch with bare hands;
  • ③ Disinfect the area and wash hands — sanitise a 30cm radius around the spot; wash hands with soap after removing gloves.

Cleaning is only step one — treatment is what matters. Rats need source work: entry-point proofing plus monitoring (see our rat control hub); roaches need colony treatment: gel baits placed around nests (see our cockroach page). Clean the droppings without treating the population, and they will be back within weeks.

❓ Droppings ID FAQ

I found one black speck — fastest way to identify?

Three checks: bigger than 6mm and rice-shaped = likely rat; pepper-sized = likely roach; then location — piles along walls are rats, sticky specks in cabinets and appliance seams are roaches. Still unsure? Upload a photo to the AI diagnostician for a 15-second report.

How dangerous are rat droppings?

Rats transmit hantavirus, salmonella and more; dried urine and droppings can infect via inhaled dust. Always spray-wet with disinfectant and wear gloves — never dry-sweep.

What are the health effects of cockroach droppings?

Frass and moults carry allergens that can trigger asthma and allergies (especially in children), and they signal a hygiene risk in kitchens — arrange colony treatment promptly.

Found rat droppings — can I just buy bait?

You can clean and disinfect first, but consumer bait often kills rats inside wall voids (odour nightmare), and without sealing entry points they return. Professional practice: survey + source-proofing + monitoring.

How do I stop droppings from reappearing?

Three things: proof entry points (drains, door gaps, pipe penetrations), manage food sources (sealed storage, night-time cleaning), and inspect regularly. Home methods in our rat-proofing guide.
