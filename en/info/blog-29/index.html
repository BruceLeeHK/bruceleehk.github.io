🥚 Why Egg Recognition Decides the Battle

Most DIY pest control fails not because the product is weak, but because it kills adults and nymphs while the eggs survive. Pest eggs have three properties that make them the pivot of any treatment:

  • Physical armour: the ootheca shell is dense — ordinary insecticide cannot penetrate it, so spraying never kills what is inside;
  • Time-lag surge: a German ootheca hatches in about 30 days, releasing 16–40 nymphs at once — two weeks of "no roaches" after spraying, then a whole new generation;
  • Hidden placement: eggs are laid deep in crevices, so "there is no nest in my flat" is usually an illusion.

This is why professional protocols (gel-bait chain-kill, 180°C steam) are designed as "wait for hatching, then kill" or "kill eggs directly with high heat" — not surface spraying alone.

🪳 Cockroach Oothecae in Depth: ID and Handling

The ootheca is the most misidentified egg stage — homeowners mistake it for "mud pellets", "seeds" or even sweets. Three ID marks:

  • Shape: a plump purse about 8mm (American up to 12mm) with ridge-like segments, colour turning from light brown when fresh to dark brown;
  • Location clue: a German cockroach female carries the ootheca until just before hatching — a female with a brown capsule on her abdomen means an outbreak is imminent; American cockroaches glue oothecae onto hidden surfaces near drains and wall voids;
  • Hatching sign: a saw-toothed split along one edge means nymphs are about to emerge — or already have.

Correct handling after a find: ① do not crush it bare-handed — crushed oothecae may scatter nymphs, and cockroach debris is a common allergen; ② seal the whole thing in a bag and discard (pour boiling water over it first for extra safety); ③ record the location — the crevice where you found it is usually the colony harbourage; place gel bait points there so newly hatched nymphs feed and poison the nest in chain; ④ watch that spot closely for two weeks for new frass or new oothecae.

🛏️ Bed Bug Eggs: 1mm Ivory Rice Grains Only Steam Kills

Bed bug eggs are salt-grain sized, ivory-white and translucent ovals with a tiny hatching cap at one end; their sticky surface glues them into bed frame wood joints, seam stitching and screw holes. A female lays 200–500 eggs in her lifetime, distributing a few per day within a 1.5-metre radius of the bed.

Bed bug eggs are highly resistant to insecticide, and their glue spots sit deep in crevices — DIY sprays rarely reach. The industry standard is 180°C dry steam advanced inch by inch: high temperature kills eggs and adults on contact with no chemical residue, suitable for mattresses, sofas and wooden furniture. Even after steaming, a 7–14 day follow-up inspection is needed to catch any missed eggs after hatching. The full process is on our deep bed bug extermination page.

⚪ Termites: You Rarely See the Eggs — Mud Tubes Are the Signal

Termite eggs stay inside nest chambers, tended by workers — in a home you almost never see them directly. Three "outer-signal" signs are far more practical:

  • Mud tubes (shelter tubes): thin mud lines along skirting, door frames and false ceilings — termite "highways" connecting nest to food;
  • Alates (swarmers): in late spring (especially humid pre-rain evenings, April–June) winged reproductives swarm; a pile of translucent shed wings on the windowsill suggests a new colony nearby;
  • Hollow knock sound: door or window frames sounding hollow when tapped may already be gutted inside.

Hidden eggs are exactly why termite control cannot be surface spraying — a microwave scanner (T3i) first maps the nest and tube network, then the colony is eliminated directly. See our T3i microwave detection case study.

🧯 Three Principles for Any Egg Find: Do Not Crush, Seal, Trace the Source

Whatever egg or ootheca you find, the handling logic is identical — three principles:

  • ① Never crush bare-handed — a crushed ootheca may scatter nymphs, and debris is a known allergen; lift with sticky tape or seal whole in a bag;
  • ② Seal, discard, and record — photographing the location and count gives you the data to gauge infestation scale later;
  • ③ Trace the source, not just the eggs — eggs are the result, not the cause. Find the harbourage (cabinet voids, bed frame, drain, tube network) and treat it; clearing eggs alone brings a new batch in two weeks.
"Clearing ten eggs achieves less than sealing one source — egg removal stops the bleeding; source control ends the war."

❓ 常見問題 FAQ

I found a cockroach ootheca at home — what now?

Do not crush it bare-handed. Seal it in a bag and discard (boiling water first for extra safety), and record where you found it — that crevice is usually the colony harbourage. Place gel bait points nearby so newly hatched nymphs feed and poison the nest in chain; watch for two weeks for new frass or oothecae. For larger finds, call a professional directly.

How many nymphs hatch from one ootheca?

A German cockroach ootheca holds 30–40 eggs on average; American 14–16. A German female produces 4–8 oothecae in her lifetime — theoretically over a hundred descendants from one female. Finding an ootheca means you are ahead of the outbreak — always act.

Can I just squash the ootheca?

Not bare-handed. A crushed capsule may scatter un-hatched nymphs, and cockroach debris is a common allergen (especially for children and people with rhinitis). Sticky tape, a sealed bag or boiling water are all better options.

Are bed bug eggs visible to the naked eye?

Yes, but easy to miss — about 1mm, ivory-white translucent, glued into frame seams, wood joints and screw holes. Use your phone torch close to the frame and scan inch by inch; clusters of tiny ivory grains are highly suspicious. If confirmed, skip DIY spray (it scatters them) — high-temperature steam is the standard egg-killing method.

Why do I still see roaches after spraying?

Because insecticide cannot reach the eggs inside oothecae, and repellent formulations drive colonies to scatter — two weeks later the eggs hatch all at once, creating the "sprayed and now there are more" effect. The correct logic is: no repellence, feed them instead — gel bait points let nymphs and adults feed and poison the nest in chain, clearing the egg-stage population together.
