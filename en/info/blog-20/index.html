FEHD 2024 Hong Kong District Rat Data Revealed: Yau Tsim Mong Tops 20,000 Rats — the "Dead Rat Stench" Crisis Explained

The Food and Environmental Hygiene Department (FEHD) has published the 2024 count of dead rats collected and live rats captured across 18 districts: a territory-wide total of 122,878 rats, with Yau Tsim Mong topping the chart at 20,678 and Sham Shui Po close behind at 12,348. This is the most authoritative snapshot of Hong Kong's rat problem available today.   • No.1 Yau Tsim Mong (20,678): the highest eatery density in Hong Kong — Mong Kok markets, the Yau Ma Tei Fruit Market and Tsim Sha Tsui kitchens running day and night feed colonies of Norway rats; mixed commercial-residential blocks let rats climb vertically along grease-trap and soil lines. 3. The "dead rat stench" crisis: the warning hidden in the official figures

Note the official wording: "dead rats collected and live rats captured". A large share of those dead rats traces back to traditional rat poison. A poisoned rat does not die on the spot — it typically returns to its nest first: restaurant ceilings, tang lau corners, wall voids, deep drainage. That triggers a four-stage crisis:

  • Stench invades the home: a decomposing rat smells for two to three weeks, and sealed ceiling voids simply cannot vent the odour.
  • Maggot outbreak: flies, maggots and mites feeding on the carcass crawl out through gaps and spread across the home, re-contaminating kitchens and food.
  • Bacterial spread: decomposition releases bacteria and odour molecules — a real health risk for children, the elderly and pets.
  • A long, costly cleanup: locating the carcass can mean opening ceilings or walls; cleanup and disinfection can cost more than the original treatment.

That is why professional technicians refuse to "lay bait and wait for the bodies". We advocate infrared night-route tracking combined with IPM rodent-mesh sealing: stop live rats at the source and no rat ever dies inside your home — a truly non-toxic, permanent fix.

4. The expert rat-proofing plan: from data to action

So what should hotspot residents do? The professional three-step plan:

  • Step 1 — Source sealing: close every entry point (gullies, pipe penetrations, door bases) with stainless steel rodent mesh — any gap wider than 6 mm is a rat doorway.
  • Step 2 — Physical trapping: place traps flush along walls, parallel to rat runways, and beat neophobia — a non-toxic approach that is safe around children and pets.
  • Step 3 — Long-term prevention: cut off food and water (sealed waste, pet food stored away, decluttering) and schedule inspections so the problem never returns.

5. District rat data FAQ

Q1: Which district had the most rats in 2024?
Per the FEHD 2024 figures: Yau Tsim Mong ranks first with 20,678 rats (dead collected + live captured), followed by Sham Shui Po (12,348), Wong Tai Sin (10,870), Kowloon City (9,569) and Wan Chai (7,650). The territory-wide total across 18 districts is 122,878.

Q2: A dead rat smells inside my ceiling or wall — what should I do?
Use activated charcoal or coffee grounds as a temporary odour absorber and keep the area ventilated, then arrange professional carcass location, removal and disinfection as soon as possible — opening ceilings yourself can spread maggots through the home. Prevention beats cure: choose a non-toxic plan of source sealing plus physical trapping and no rat will ever die indoors again.

Q3: Does the FEHD treat private homes?
The FEHD is responsible for rodent control in public places (streets, back lanes, public markets). Rodent problems inside private homes, village houses, restaurants and commercial units generally require a licensed private pest control company. To report rats in public places, call the FEHD hotline 2868 0000.

Q4: Why do professionals advise against rat poison?
Poisoned rats usually die inside ceilings, wall voids or drainage, causing the stench and maggot crisis — the very source of the "dead rats" in the official statistics. Bait also poses poisoning risks to children and pets. The modern professional approach is night-route tracking, stainless mesh source sealing and physical trapping — non-toxic and permanent.

6. Technician's verdict: let the data move you — act early

A territory-wide total of 122,878 rats in 2024 is a warning: a rat problem is never "someone else's business" — it can start in your own flat. Residents of the big five hotspots — Yau Tsim Mong, Sham Shui Po, Wong Tai Sin, Kowloon City and Wan Chai — should act immediately at the first sign of droppings, gnaw marks or night-time scratching: the longer an infestation is left, the faster it multiplies and the higher the sealing cost and health risk climb.
