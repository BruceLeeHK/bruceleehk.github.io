As international travel fully rebounds, the global spread of bed bugs (also known as wood lice) is heating up again! Recent overseas reports of bed bug incidents — from long-haul flights to leisure venues in South Korea, a favourite destination for Hong Kong travellers — spare no one. For Hong Kong people preparing to travel, how to avoid bites during the flight, and worse still, how to avoid bringing these 'souvenirs' home inside your luggage, has become one of the hottest hygiene concerns. This article rounds up international media reports and expert advice: the golden rules of travel bed bug prevention, plus the local Hong Kong bed bug treatment guide.

✈️ Fright at 35,000 Feet: Bed Bug Panic on a Qantas Long-haul Flight

According to international media reports including The Sydney Morning Herald, on a Qantas Boeing 787 Dreamliner flying from Rome to Perth, multiple economy-class passengers reported being repeatedly bitten by unknown insects during the nearly 17-hour flight. Although cabin crew provided antihistamines to relieve the itching, demand far outstripped supply and the medication soon ran out.

Reports say that after landing, Qantas confirmed to international media that bed bugs were found in a small number of economy-class seats. The aircraft was immediately grounded and underwent a full deep-clean disinfection. The incident shows just how strong bed bugs' ability to cling and spread is — even international flights with strict cleaning standards can become a transmission vehicle via passengers' luggage. Next time you take a long-haul flight, pay attention to bed bug prevention around your seat and luggage.

♨️ HK Travellers' Hotspot: 'Collective Nests' Found in a Busan Jjimjilbang

Meanwhile, according to reports by local media and insect-education YouTubers, South Korea — a firm favourite among HK travellers — has sounded the bed bug alarm again. Reports say the team received tips from the public and visited a well-known jjimjilbang in the Yeongdo district of Busan to investigate.

Footage shows live bed bugs crawling all over the seams of resting mats, wooden wall cracks and dark corners where customers commonly lie down; investigators even found 'collective nests' of eggs, nymphs and adult bed bugs gathering in the gaps of wooden structures, with visible red bite marks on several sleeping customers. Reports say local authorities stepped in immediately after the exposure and ordered the venue to disinfect thoroughly. Shared-bed venues like jjimjilbangs and hotels are exactly the high-risk environments for bed bug transmission — stay extra alert when travelling.

🧳 Expert Tips: Four Golden Rules of Travel 'Bed Bug & Bite Prevention'

Bed bugs breed fast and can survive months without feeding. To reduce the chance of your trip being affected, we recommend the following four precautions when travelling:

1. Thorough check before settling in (Check-in habit): When entering a hotel room or jjimjilbang resting area, never put your luggage down straight away. First check mattress corners, pillow seams, carpets and dark corners of wooden furniture for black blood stains (bed bug droppings) or live bugs.

2. Keep luggage off the floor: Place your suitcase on a metal luggage rack or smooth surface whenever possible; avoid leaning it against walls or putting it on carpets, and keep the zippers closed at all times.

3. High-heat treatment for clothing: After returning to Hong Kong, wash clothing and bedding at 60°C or above and tumble-dry on high heat as soon as possible; for items that cannot take high heat, seal them in a plastic bag and leave them in a freezer at -18°C or below for 3 to 4 days to deal with the eggs.

4. Luggage defence line before entering home: Before entering, check the wheels and gaps of your suitcase outside the door or in the bathroom, to avoid carrying bugs into the bedroom.

🏠 What If I Accidentally Bring Bed Bugs Home?

Bed bugs are extremely good at hiding. Once inside, they breed quickly on bed frames, skirting boards and sofas.

⚠️ Special reminder: never blindly reach for ordinary insecticide spray! Common aerosol insecticides carry volatile, pungent chemicals; they can hardly reach bed bug eggs hidden deep in crevices, and may instead disturb the colony, driving bed bugs to scatter to other rooms and making the situation worse.

When you notice rows or clusters of itchy red bites on your body, or dark blood spots on your bedsheets, consult a professional pest control company as soon as possible for integrated pest management (IPM) using high-temperature steam and professional residual agents. ❓ Bed Bug FAQ

How do I check a hotel room for bed bugs?

Never put your luggage down straight after entering. Check mattress corners and piping, pillow and pillowcase folds, behind the headboard, carpet edges and dark corners of wooden furniture for black blood stains (bed bug droppings), shed skins, eggs or live bugs. If in doubt, ask the hotel for another room, keep your suitcase zipped up and off the floor.

Back in Hong Kong — what if I suspect I brought bed bugs home?

Wash clothing and bedding at 60°C or above and tumble-dry on high heat as soon as possible; for items that cannot take heat, seal them in a plastic bag and freeze at -18°C or below for 3 to 4 days to deal with the eggs. Check suitcase gaps and wheels, and watch for dark blood spots on sheets — if you find any signs, arrange a professional inspection early; the longer an infestation drags on, the harder and more costly it usually becomes.

Can I handle bed bugs myself with ordinary insecticide spray?

Not recommended. Ordinary aerosol insecticides are volatile and pungent; they can hardly reach bed bug eggs hidden deep in crevices and may instead disturb the colony, driving the bugs to scatter into other rooms and making the situation harder to handle. Professional practice is integrated pest management (IPM) combining high-temperature steam with professional residual agents, followed up according to the site conditions.
