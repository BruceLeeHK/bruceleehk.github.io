Executive Summary

Hong Kong is dense, warm and humid, and the pest profile of each of its 18 districts varies dramatically with building density and the age of the housing stock. Traditionally, the public has relied on the Food and Environmental Hygiene Department (FEHD) "Rodent Reference Index" and the Gravitrap/Ovitrap Index to gauge risk. Yet official data primarily reflects the hygiene of public streets, back lanes and markets — it measures what happens outside your property, not inside it.

This white paper is compiled by Hong Kong professional pest-control brand Pest Control Master (CEDARS_LTD). Combining the latest FEHD public data with frontline survey insights gathered through IoT smart bait boxes and Termatrac T3i radar detectors across private residences, commercial towers, educational institutions and village houses, it is the first report to close the data blind spot between "public space" and "indoor / commercial premises". Whether you manage property, run an F&B or retail business, own a flat from overseas or live in a village house, this report helps you re-assess your district's true pest risk through a dual lens: the government measures the street; we measure your rooms.

1. What Is the "Index Gap" Between Official Data and Real Indoor Infestations?

Direct answer: According to FEHD's historical data, some new towns and upmarket districts record relatively low public rodent and mosquito indices — yet that does not mean the properties there are free of pest threats. Official indices measure public space, while the real invasion routes run inside buildings. This divergence between "low public index" and "high indoor risk" is what this white paper names the Index Gap. Our frontline AI diagnosis system and survey data reveal three hidden crises:

The "Cross-Interpretation Method" — the government measures the street; we measure your rooms. FEHD indices reflect public hygiene in streets, back lanes and markets; CEDARS_LTD field surveys reflect the indoor reality of private homes, commercial towers and village houses. Wherever the two curves diverge, hidden infestations are signalling.

🏢 Hidden Crisis 1: Three-Dimensional Invasion — indices monitor the ground; rats travel the "sky corridors"

FEHD's rodent index monitors ground level, but rats routinely travel via external drainage pipes, air-conditioner casings, false ceilings and server-room cable trays — a three-dimensional movement network that reaches high-floor shops and residences directly. This explains why a tower with spotless back lanes can still find roof rats in a 20th-floor server room: a ground-based index is structurally blind to the "vertical city" of rat runs.

💧 Hidden Crisis 2: Microclimate Breeding — however thorough street mosquito control is, chiller rooms still breed

No matter how thorough street-level mosquito control is, the water tanks of village-house gardens and pooled water in shopping-mall chiller rooms form perfect "microclimates" for larvae. Gravitraps are deployed in public space and cannot cover these enclosed, stable water bodies inside properties — mosquito risk has always been "one household, one world".

✈️ Hidden Crisis 3: International Transmission — bed bugs and termites follow logistics, not hygiene

Bed bugs and termites have no direct link to outdoor hygiene. They are carried indoors through e-commerce logistics, overseas travel and renovation timber, making them the number-one hidden threat in upmarket residential districts. No municipal hygiene index can stop a suitcase or a shipment of imported wood — which is precisely why bed-bug complaints dominate premium housing estates.

Direct answer: In 2026, allocate your pest-control budget in this order — structural exclusion (Exclusion) first, upgraded audit compliance (Compliance) second, and an AI rapid-reporting mechanism third. Details below:

FAQ — 18-District Pest Risk & White Paper Explained

What is the FEHD Rodent Reference Index, and what does it actually reflect?

The Rodent Reference Index is a public rodent indicator calculated by the Food and Environmental Hygiene Department from bait-consumption success rates, reflecting rodent activity in public spaces such as streets, back lanes and markets. It does not cover indoor infestations in private residences, commercial towers or village houses — the data blind spot this white paper closes with the Cross-Interpretation Method.

Why do areas with a low rodent index still get rats indoors?

Because the index monitors ground-level public space, while rats move three-dimensionally through external pipes, AC casings, false ceilings and cable trays, reaching high-floor units directly. A low public index does not mean low indoor risk — the divergence is the Index Gap.

Which parts of Hong Kong carry the highest pest risk?

By cluster: the Old Urban Core (Sham Shui Po, Yau Tsim Mong, Kowloon City) tops the rodent index; the New Territories & Rural Belt (Yuen Long, Sha Tin, Sai Kung, Tai Po) faces wet-season mosquitoes plus termites and fire ants around village houses; the Commercial & Premium Residential Belt (Central & Western, Tseung Kwan O, Tung Chung) sees bed bugs and roof rats as the top threats. Risk type matters more than a single ranking.

How do rats reach high floors? I thought rats were a ground-floor problem?

Rats are expert climbers. They travel along external drainage pipes, AC casings and false ceilings, so offices and flats above the 20th floor are equally exposed. Roof rats entering server rooms via cable trays are among the most common invasion paths in commercial towers.

Are bed bugs caused by poor hygiene? Why do premium estates get them?

Bed-bug transmission has no direct link to hygiene levels — they arrive through parcels and furniture logistics, overseas travel luggage and domestic-helper turnover. That is exactly why bed bugs top the complaint charts in upmarket districts with excellent public hygiene scores: international transmission at work.

Does termite treatment always require breaking open walls or renovation?

No. With the Termatrac T3i radar detector we precisely locate termite activity and hidden nests behind timber without damaging wall finishes — diagnose first, treat second, and skip the blind demolition.

What is the difference between a traditional bait box and an IoT smart bait box?

A traditional bait box needs manual periodic checks and cannot report in real time. An IoT smart bait box provides 24/7 cloud infrared sensing and precisely logs peak rodent hours, enabling interception before an infestation instead of remediation after it — ideal for school server rooms and supermarket warehouses.

What is IPM (Integrated Pest Management), and how is it different from ordinary pest control?

IPM follows the sequence monitoring - diagnosis - physical exclusion - targeted treatment - audit review, prioritising physical and source-level control with chemicals only as the last resort. Blind-spray pest control skips diagnosis and exclusion: pests die in the short term but keep returning.

How should property managers and businesses allocate a 2026 pest-control budget?

In priority order: first, structural exclusion — a one-time investment and the only measure that ends the problem; second, audit compliance — bilingual IPM reports meeting HACCP/ISO 22000; third, an AI rapid-reporting mechanism so frontline staff can photograph and diagnose instantly, with intervention inside 24 hours.
