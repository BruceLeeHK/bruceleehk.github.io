How bad is Hong Kong's rat problem — and why act now?

Hong Kong's warm, humid climate keeps rats active all year with explosive breeding potential, and any gap wider than 6 mm is a doorway. Season changes, heavy rain and nearby construction all drive rats indoors. The risks go far beyond nuisance:

  • 🔥 Chewed cables = fire hazard — Rats must gnaw constantly — ceiling wiring and air-con cables are their favourites; fires attributed to rodent damage occur every year.
  • 🦠 Disease transmission — Rodent waste can spread leptospirosis and hantavirus; rat fleas and mites travel indoors with them.
  • 🍕 Food contamination & damage — Kitchen supplies, furniture, documents and stock — nothing is safe; restaurants reported for infestation risk closure.

Our core methodology is IPM: assess first (species, routes, sources), seal second (stainless steel mesh), monitor third (IoT smart rat boxes) — turning "endless killing" into "cannot get in".

Why do rats keep coming back no matter what?

Three reasons: ① kill-but-don't-seal — drainage and entry points stay open, so new rats replace old ones; ② neophobia — rats avoid newly placed traps and glue boards for days; ③ food sources remain (kitchen waste, pet food, clutter). Our sequence: track routes → seal sources → clean the environment.

Is rat poison necessary? Any non-toxic options?

Our core programme is non-toxic: night tracking, physical trapping and source sealing — completely safe for pets and children. Poison's biggest problem is rats dying inside ceilings or voids; the stench and maggots that follow are the real nightmare. Indoor baiting is a last resort.

Is there a warranty on proofing works? What if rats get in again?

Proofing works carry a warranty (by scale of works); if rats re-enter through the same treated positions during the warranty, re-inspection and re-sealing are free. Maintenance-contract clients also receive scheduled inspections and IoT monitoring reports — problems surface before outbreaks.
