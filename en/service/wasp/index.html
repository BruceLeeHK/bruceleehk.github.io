Wasp Nest Safety Principles — ID First, Environment-friendly

Nest removal is more than "knock it down". Species, gear, timing and prevention — all four matter:

  • Species ID First — Wasps (paper nests, aggressive) and bees (key pollinators) need completely different handling; beehives are referred for collection rather than killed.
  • Professional Protective Gear — Bee suits + extension tools for high and enclosed positions, reducing handling risk.
  • Timing Selection — Handled during lower wasp-activity hours, with foot-traffic management, to reduce attack risk.
  • Re-nesting Prevention — Post-removal gap sealing, residual removal and seasonal checks reduce repeat nesting and repeat chemical use.

Wasp Nest Removal FAQ

Found a nest at home or in the garden — what now?

Do not knock it, hose it or burn it. Keep your distance, photograph it (no flash) and WhatsApp us — the team assesses species and position before any handling. Disturbed colonies turn aggressive; DIY handling is extremely risky.

How much does wasp nest removal cost?

It depends on nest size, height and foot-traffic risk — we do not publish a flat rate. Every case is quoted in writing after survey with no surprise add-ons; WhatsApp photos get a free initial assessment first.

Wasp vs bee — and why does it matter?

Wasps mostly build paper nests and are aggressive; bees build honeycombs and are key pollinators. Confirmed beehives are referred to beekeepers for collection rather than killed; removal applies to wasp nests.

Will the nest come back after removal?

Old nests are not reused, but a suitable spot (eave gaps, wall cavities) may attract new nesting. After removal we provide re-nesting prevention advice: gap sealing, residual removal and seasonal checks.

When is wasp season?

Active in spring and summer, with colonies largest in late summer and early autumn. Checking canopies, AC tops and garden trees after typhoons and heavy rain is a good habit — early nests are small and simpler to handle.

Do you handle high nests on village canopies?

Yes, with professional bee suits and extension tools. High-risk positions (beside switch boxes, enclosed spots, busy areas) are assessed before work. Note: nests in common areas (estate podiums, facades) fall under IO/management duties — we coordinate with the management office before handling.
