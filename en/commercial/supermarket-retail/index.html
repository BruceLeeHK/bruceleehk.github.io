Four Major Pest Control Points — Precisely Targeted

Supermarket environments are complex, and each zone demands a different approach. We survey by zone and strike the four highest-risk areas one by one:

🔴 A. Hot Food Counters & Bakery (Rodent Hotspot)

The challenge：The rich food aromas from pastry rooms, bakeries and hot snack counters make these the prime target for rodent activity and intrusion — a single sighting can drag down the whole store’s food safety rating.

Our solution：We deploy discreet night-time service with stainless steel mesh source-proofing, supported by safe baits and rodent-proofing devices — cutting off rodent entry paths into food handling zones at the root.

🔴 B. Produce & Fresh Zones (Cockroach & Fruit Fly Breeding Grounds)

The challenge：Pests hitch-hike into stores inside fruits, vegetables, cartons and delivery packaging. German cockroaches love shelf and chiller gaps, while fruit flies breed rapidly wherever produce accumulates.

Our solution：We run regular density monitoring with AFCD-registered low-toxicity gel baits and granules, plus produce-zone-specific trapping devices (such as safe fruit fly cups) for precise, localised control that keeps food and the environment safe.

🔴 C. Fish, Meat Zones & Refuse Rooms (Flies & Drain Flies)

The challenge：Drains, sewage gaps and refuse rooms accumulate organic residue — the main breeding and activity grounds for flies, fruit flies and moth flies, directly affecting the shopping experience.

Our solution：Larvae typically breed in drain gaps, so we provide biological drain treatments, glue-board UV fly lamps and targeted residual spraying for refuse rooms — restoring hygiene and interrupting larval development.

🔴 D. Back-of-House Storage & Loading Docks (Rodent Damage Zone)

The challenge：Warehouse racking and loading docks are high-activity rodent zones where gnawed packaging, contamination and reputational losses happen fastest.

Our solution：We install IoT smart rat stations with 24/7 cloud sensing in warehouses and loading areas, paired with physical rodent-proof boards and stainless steel mesh — keeping structural gaps sealed and every pest monitored long-term.

Commercial clients need more than "spray and go" — they need an auditable management system. Our supermarket IPM programme runs on eight documented steps:

Our specialist visits the site, meets the person in charge and reviews operational and environmental details, with AI photo diagnosis assisting pest identification.

Step 2Density survey

Detailed pest density and species survey before any treatment, pinpointing infested locations and population sizes.

Step 3IPM monitoring point map

Step 6Bilingual IPM service report

After every service, a detailed report compliant with HACCP / ISO22000 / FEHD licensing standards, assessing pest status and recommending hygiene and structural improvements.

Step 7QC supervisor follow-up

Dedicated QC staff conduct regular follow-up visits to keep service quality up to standard.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can pest control be done while the store is trading?

Yes. Our programmes are designed around "discreet night-time service": we arrive after closing and finish restoration before opening. For urgent daytime cases we use low-disturbance methods (gel bait injection, fruit fly cup placement) applied locally, never disrupting shoppers or food prep. The site is cleaned before we leave, so trading resumes normally next morning.

What is a fruit fly cup? Does it affect customers?

A fruit fly cup is a compact trapping device designed for produce zones: a safe attractant lures fruit flies inside for physical capture — no harsh chemicals involved. They are small, discreet, placed under shelves or in back areas, and largely invisible to customers while delivering significant population control. It is a standard IPM tool used by international supermarket groups.

What documents do you provide for HACCP audits?

After every service you receive a bilingual IPM report (checkpoints, treatment locations, monitoring data, follow-up recommendations), the monitoring point map, IPM prescription sheets and the agent list with AFCD registration numbers. Everything is planned, recorded and traceable — mapping directly to HACCP / ISO22000 pest management clauses.

Why are German cockroaches so hard to eliminate in supermarkets?

German cockroaches breed fast, stay small and hide inside chiller motor gaps and shelf joints — conventional spraying just scatters them. We combine species identification + gel bait injection + density monitoring: baits placed in gaps are carried back to kill the colony, while each monitoring cycle re-tunes bait placement to keep the population down.

How is it priced? Can multiple stores share one contract?

Quoted by store area, zone count, risk and frequency — survey first, written quotation after, itemised. Chains can consolidate stores under a single contract: unified service standards and report formats let head office review monitoring data and follow-up progress across all branches.

How often is service provided?

Typically monthly for standard stores; higher-risk sites (dense hot food counters, ageing drains, refuse rooms near residences) may move to fortnightly; IoT stations monitor 24/7 — issues are flagged before customers complain. Final frequency follows the post-survey recommendation.
