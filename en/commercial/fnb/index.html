Four Major F&B Black Spots — Precisely Targeted

Kitchens and beverage counters in cha chaan tengs and bing sutt are compact and packed with equipment, running hot and humid for years with accumulating grease and sugar — near-perfect breeding conditions for pests. The interior structure is complex: cable conduits, false-ceiling voids and wall cavities criss-cross the premises, giving rodents, cockroaches and flying insects ample shelter and movement space. Once an infestation takes hold it is extremely difficult to eradicate — and it can quickly trigger customer complaints, FEHD prosecutions or even a suspension order.

🔴 A. Kitchen & Cooking Range (Cockroach Hot Zone)

Challenge: German cockroaches love to hide in the gaps of fridges motors, door seals, deep fryers and dishwashers — and can ride straight out to the table with a dish, sparking customer complaints and a reputation crisis.

Solution: During non-trading hours we apply AFCD-registered low-toxicity cockroach gel bait with granular bait in precise pin-point placements; the effect spreads as cockroaches carry it back to the nest, delivering systematic colony elimination without disturbing daytime kitchen operations.

🔴 B. Beverage Counter, Milk Tea & Dessert Station (Fruit Flies & Small Flies)

Challenge: Under-counter fridge bases, evaporated-milk and syrup residue and condensate lines on ice wells readily attract fruit flies and midges right beside the dining and drink area.

Solution: We deploy dedicated fruit-fly trapping devices at flight hotspots together with glue-board UV fly lamps (avoiding electric-zapper fragments contaminating food), keeping the customer dining area clean and presentable.

🔴 C. Toilets & Rear-lane Drains (Flies & Drain Flies)

Challenge: Toilets and rear lanes carry heavy organic load in the drains — a breeding ground for fly and drain-fly (moth fly) larvae.

Solution: We apply biological drain treatment at the source inside drain gaps, combined with professional residual spraying around refuse rooms and rear lanes, cutting off larval development and keeping you compliant with FEHD hygiene standards.

🔴 D. False Ceilings, Rear Conduits & Dry Stores (Rodents & Entry Points)

Challenge: Rats typically enter through rear-lane cable conduits and ceiling voids, gnawing and contaminating rice, flour and other raw ingredients — and chewing through wiring to create fire risks.

Solution: Our officers carry out source-level physical exclusion with stainless-steel rodent mesh and gnaw-resistant sealant over cable penetrations and pipe gaps; IoT smart rodent stations with 24/7 cloud sensing are installed in dry stores and false ceilings to trace entry paths in real time and trap with precision.

Restaurant Pest Control FAQ

What does restaurant rodent control need to satisfy FEHD?

FEHD inspectors look for signs of pests, control measures and records. Our programme is aligned with FEHD guidance: source exclusion + IoT monitoring + scheduled inspections, with records issued per visit — documents ready at inspection time, not just verbal assurance.

What is HACCP pest control?

HACCP (Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points) is a food safety management system in which pest control is one control point. HACCP-grade service means planned, recorded and traceable control — we provide service records, IPM treatment prescriptions and monitoring data so your restaurant can face HACCP audits with confidence.

Can you work in a restaurant while it trades?

Yes — we schedule around closing or off-peak hours; kitchen areas use low-disruption methods such as gel bait and non-toxic bait rather than large-scale spraying, and we clean up so the next day's prep is unaffected.

Is non-toxic bait really safe?

Food-safe non-toxic bait contains no highly toxic actives — it works through physical capture plus attractant formulation, so placing it near kitchens and food storage does not create contamination risk. That is precisely why it fits HACCP food safety requirements.

How is it priced?

By floor area, risk and frequency — no flat rate card; survey first, written quotation. Monthly contracts include inspections, IoT monitoring, bait refresh and records, itemised for easy comparison.

How often should service visits be?

Typically monthly; high-risk settings (refuse rooms near residences, food courts) may move to fortnightly; IoT rodent boxes monitor 24/7 so issues surface early, not at complaint time. Actual frequency follows the post-survey recommendation.
